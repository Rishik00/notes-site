# **Lost in Backpropagation: The LM Head is a Gradient Bottleneck**

# **One-line takeaway**

The last layer is important. But also a pain in the ass.

# **Problem**&#x20;

The current softmax bottleneck talks about the loss in information from the vocab space (embedding, de-embedding matrices) and the model's dimension (activation matrices). Truth is: there might be another perspective from which this can be viewed to get the full story.

# **Core idea**&#x20;

To view the softmax bottleneck in the optimization perspective. The authors state that a sufficiently large gap between the activation space and vocab space (called $D$ and $V$, which makes me feel uncomfortable) yields suboptimal updates during gradient descent. They train toy dense language models up to 2B params on 11B tokens from FineWeb and analyze the trajectories with different hyperparameter configurations.

# **Method**

## *Stage-1: Confusing sigma for softmax (and establishing known facts)*

Assume the standard transformer's objective to be

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta,X)
=-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{s,t}
\left\langle
\mathbf{1}_{x_{s,t}},
\log\sigma\!\left(W_\theta\varphi_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{s,<t})\right)
\right\rangle.
$$

Here, $\sigma$ is softmax, $W_\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times D}$ is the LM-head projection matrix, $\varphi_\theta$ maps contexts to hidden representations in $\mathbb{R}^D$, and $\mathbf{1}_{x_{s,t}}$ is the one-hot vector for token $x_{s,t}$. The observed dataset is

$$
X=(x_{s,t})_{s=1,\dots,S;\,t=1,\dots,L_s}.
$$

Prior research (Yang et al., 2018) gives a matrix form for the same objective:

$$
\mathcal{L}
=-\frac{1}{T}
\left\langle
N,
\log\sigma\!\left(H_\theta W_\theta^\top\right)
\right\rangle_F.
$$

Here, the next-token count vector for a context $c\in\mathcal{C}$ is

$$
N_c
=\sum_{s,t}
\delta_{c=\mathbf{x}_{s,<t}}\,
\mathbf{1}_{x_{s,t}}
\in\mathbb{N}^V.
$$

The paper doesn't talk a lot about $N$, so let me. $N_c$ is the target word-count distribution for a given context, computed using the indicator $\delta_{c=\mathbf{x}_{s,<t}}$, which selects all positions where context $c$ appears. The row-normalized form $\tilde{N}$ gives the empirical next-token probability distribution for each context.

## *Stage-2: Expressivity math*

We consider Gibbs' inequality, which states that the loss has a lower bound established by the normalized probabilities:

$$
\mathcal{L}
\geq
-\frac{1}{T}
\left\langle N,\log\tilde{N}\right\rangle_F.
$$

Equality holds if and only if $\sigma(H_\theta W_\theta^\top)=\tilde{N}$.

Then they use the ranks of the hidden-state and vocabulary matrices to establish that the logit matrix has rank at most $D$:

$$
\operatorname{rank}\!\left(H_\theta W_\theta^\top\right)\leq D,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}\!\left(\log\sigma(H_\theta W_\theta^\top)\right)\leq D+1.
$$

BUT, there's something interesting that they state:

$$
\left|
\sigma(H_iW^\top)_{\operatorname*{arg\,max}(\tilde{N}_i)}
-\tilde{N}_{i,\operatorname*{arg\,max}(\tilde{N}_i)}
\right|
<\varepsilon.
$$

The above equation states that the top-1 probabilities will not be affected when $D\geq2$, hence preventing the problem from affecting greedy decoding.

## *Stage-3: Gradient descent.*

The LM-head update rule is defined as follows:

$$
W_\theta^{\tau+1}
=W_\theta^\tau
-\eta\,(P_\theta^\tau-\tilde{N})^\top
\operatorname{diag}(f)H_\theta^\tau.
$$

The logits are $L_\theta^\tau=H_\theta^\tau(W_\theta^\tau)^\top$. The corresponding rescaled update direction is

$$
\Delta_\theta^\tau
=\frac{L_\theta^{\tau+1}-L_\theta^\tau}{\eta},
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(\Delta_\theta^\tau)\leq2D.
$$

The rank of the prediction error is bounded below by

$$
\operatorname{rank}(P-\tilde{N})
\geq
\min\!\left(|\mathcal{C}^{uu}|,V-1\right)
:=\mathcal{D}^u(N).
$$

They state, and I quote: Informally, this says that as long as many tokens each appear as the unique observed continuation of some context, the prediction error has very high rank, close to $V$.

And they quote that the rank of this matrix is closer to $V$ than $D$. Fuck!

They take this a step further by writing an analogue to the Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem, which states that for the Frobenius norm (or another unitarily invariant norm), the SVD delivers the best low-rank approximation:

$$
\left\lVert
\Delta_\theta^\tau-(P_\theta^\tau-\tilde{N})
\right\rVert_F
>
\sqrt{
\sum_{i=2D+1}^{\mathcal{D}^u(N)}\varsigma_i^2
}
>0.
$$

The above equation states that the logit gradient has intrinsic rank greater than $2D$, and no gradient-descent step through a rank-$D$ LM head can match it. Hence, they state and sort of prove that the LM head is bottlenecking the gradients.

# **Results**

They run a very simple experiment on a 2B model (dense, Llama-3 style) with their own invented language (sampled down from FineWeb-Edu). Their results reveal something interesting: For a fixed $D=576$ and varying vocab sizes and learning rates, at higher vocab sizes the model's validation loss (the perf measure here) is VERY BAD. This sorta proves empirically that their theory is valid.

# **Limitations**

1. THE LANGUAGE IS SYNTHETIC. idk what to do with that.&#x20;
2. We don't know if this applies to real world situations. I want to understand what would happen if the same technique is applied to other architectures.&#x20;
3. The experiments feel...inadequate. The theoretical analysis was very thorough but experiments felt lacking or I feel fails to sufficiently prove the claims.&#x20;
4. They under explored other optimizers. As far as I know they haven't studied the effects of second order optimizers on such models.
