# Fractal basins trap latent reasoning

## One-line takeaway

A dynamical systems view on latent reasoning models (NOT LLMs, but maybe can be tried for LLMs if the math allows for it)&#x20;

## Problem

Apparently reasoning models such as TRMs, looped transformers, HRMs and equilibrium reasoning models get stuck on very hard tasks or take too many iterations to solve a problem. There's no rigorous explanation to why this is happening.&#x20;

## Core idea

To view the latent reasoning process as a continuous dynamical system and to analyse it's transient states. The authors provide analysis on existing reasoning models while also training a looped transformer/parcae style model on a linear dynamical system.&#x20;

## Method and Results

#### But before we start, some terms

* Fractal basins, idk what they are. Apparently they form due to transient chaos, the phenomenon responsible for extended transients in high-dimensional dynamical systems
* Lyapunnov indicator is apparently thought of as a measure between the trajectories taken by different dynamical systems given initial state and final state.&#x20;
* Basin entropy apparently helps distinguish self similar fractal basins from random or smooth basins.&#x20;
* Plinko toy refers to high dimensional dynamics that start with close initial conditions but scatter to wildly different saddle points that are weakly-unstable.&#x20;

#### Probing existing models on puzzles

The following models were probed on sudoku, arc-agi and math puzzles: (1) TRMs (2) Parcae (3) Looped transformers.&#x20;

* Their analysis found that basin entropy strongly correlates with the number of iterations during reasoning.
* &#x20;Trajectories where  the frequency of the answers changes very quickly also faces large lyapunnov indicators.&#x20;

#### A simple training recipe to find fractal basins

A looped transformer is trained to solve a simple linear integer system. And just looking at the training logs,  they find that saddles encode these dead end reasoning routes in reasoning dynamical systems (though the very term reasoning can be debated).&#x20;

During training, it was found that incorrect solutions are found for unstable solutions.&#x20;

## Limitations

* Their tests were fairly limited for the tasks.
* The tiny reasoning model for linear system training was apparently not logged properly. &#x20;

## Useful quotes

> Though reasoning models are trained to solve discrete puzzles, their fractal basins are smooth and naturalistic, resembling optical caustics or transient lifetimes in turbulence—both phenomena arising from spatial variation in the path-lengths required for a physical system to reach fixed points.

> Successfully solving a hard problem requires that all trajectories of a reasoning model eventually converge to a single fixed point—and thus that no sustained chaos occurs.

> We find that faster-converging trajectories take direct paths toward the true solution, while slower trajectories take longer, indirect routes that visit saddle points before reaching the true solution
