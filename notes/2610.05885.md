# The Optimization Landscape of Learning CompactedContext Models

## One-line takeaway

analysis of failure modes for existing compaction models and introducing a new way of doing it via a perceived architecture. Writing is not great.&#x20;

## Problem

KV cache compression is one of the hardest problems today. Apparently it's very difficult because this is an optimization problem that requires finding the right pieces to gather.&#x20;

## Core idea

They use synthetic datasets to understand the landscape of kv cache compression and introduce their own compression algorithm called Fast Diamond.&#x20;

Theoretically, we want to learn a function g that takes the current KV for a prefilled forward pass and compress it while preserving performance. They propose that rather than trying to reproduce the base performance 1:1, but to use KL divergence to closely match the performance for every question.&#x20;

## Method

They frame the compaction problem as training a small network (what they call thr perceiver) to compress the my cache. The model learns a simple set of matrices to compress the concatenation of k and v matrices.

The objective for this network is a combination between the original kv and the new compressed kv vectors passed through the model. They’re also proposing the model take the query vectors during this phase as apparently it improves performance along with the original KL on the models outputs.&#x20;

They also try to recurrently apply the linear map on itself before applying it on the kv vectors. not sure why.

They train on questions synthetically generated for passages from gutenberg books, legal books and code.&#x20;

## Results

* compared to other baselines their method preserves performance the best with 6-7% regression.
* But as we compact, it tends to just regress much more aggressively than other methods (\~92 to >10 )&#x20;
