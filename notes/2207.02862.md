# Verifying the Union of Manifolds Hypothesis for Image Data

## One-line takeaway

They put the so-called “intrinsic manifold hypothesis” into words. The theory about the connectedness of pushforward models using measure theory is genuinely awesome; the experiments are sort of a dud.

## Problem

It has been stated in many papers that a network’s latent space lies on a lower-dimensional manifold. But that is all it is—a statement; there is no formal hypothesis or verification of the statement.

## Core idea

The authors assume that the data space lies not in one manifold but in a disjoint union of many manifolds. They prove this theoretically using measure theory and pushforward properties, and empirically verify it on MNIST, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet.

## Method

The method consists of two parts: the theoretical proof that the data space is disconnected and the experiments themselves (partly covered in Results).

### 3.1 Definitions

Some of these are directly picked up from the paper, while others are simplified versions of me trying to understand why they appear.

1. **Pushforward models / pushforward DGMs** are defined by a continuous map $G: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{X}$, where $\mathcal{X}$ is the input space, $\mathcal{Z}$ is the latent space, and $G$ is the model that maps samples from $\mathbb{P}_Z$ into the input space. The paper assumes topologies on $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\mathcal{X}$ with their respective sigma-algebras.
2. A **pushforward measure** between two measurable spaces $X_1$ and $X_2$ pushes a measure from one space to the other. Mathematically:

   $$
   (G_\#\mu)(B) = \mu\bigl(G^{-1}(B)\bigr)
   \qquad \text{for all } B \in \Sigma_2,
   $$

   where $\mu$ is a measure on the first space.
3. A **sigma-algebra** on a set $X$ is a collection of subsets closed under complements, countable unions, and countable intersections.
4. Any $n$-dimensional manifold is a topological space with local neighborhoods resembling Euclidean space.
5. **Connectedness** of a manifold, in simple terms, means it cannot be broken into separate pieces by a gap. Mark this as important: it is used a lot in this paper.
6. Formally, the support of a probability distribution is the smallest closed set $C$ such that $\mathbb{P}(C) = 1$ and which represents the full probability distribution. To define support on a topological space, the corresponding sigma-algebra must be defined.
7. The **closure** of a set is the set together with all of its limit points—equivalently, the smallest closed set containing it:

   $$
   \operatorname{cl}(S) = S \cup \{\text{limit points of } S\}.
   $$

8. A **disjoint union** is a union whose elements retain a tag identifying which constituent set they came from.

### 3.2 The union of manifolds hypothesis — stated verbatim

Mathematically, it is stated as follows:

$$
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}^*)
= \bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{L} \operatorname{cl}\!\left(\mathcal{M}^{(\ell)}\right)
\subset \mathcal{X},
$$

where each $\mathcal{M}^{(\ell)}$ is a connected manifold of dimension $d^{(\ell)}$, and $\operatorname{cl}(\cdot)$ denotes closure in $\mathcal{X}$.

In other words, the support of $\mathbb{P}^*$ (the true data distribution) is a disjoint union of $L$ manifolds closed in $\mathcal{X}$. In simpler words, the true distribution’s support is disconnected.

### 3.3 Proof of the hypothesis

It comes from the following proposition.

**Proposition.** Let $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\mathcal{X}$ be topological spaces, and let $G: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{X}$ be continuous. Regard $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\mathcal{X}$ as measurable spaces with their respective sigma-algebras. Let $\mathbb{P}_Z$ be a probability measure on $\mathcal{Z}$ such that $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)$ is connected and

$$
\mathbb{P}_Z\!\left(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\right) = 1.
$$

Then $\operatorname{supp}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z)$ is connected.

In simpler words: given the data and latent spaces $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Z}$, their sigma-algebras, and a continuous map between them, the support of the pushforward of the latent distribution remains connected.

The proof was further simplified to prove only that

$$
\operatorname{supp}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z)
= \operatorname{cl}\!\left(G\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\bigr)\right),
$$

and then state that the closure of a connected set is connected.

**Step 1.** Verify that

$$
\operatorname{cl}\!\left(G\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\bigr)\right)
\subseteq \operatorname{supp}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z).
$$

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a collection of subsets $C$ of $G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z$ such that

$$
G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z(C) = 1.
$$

By the definition of pushforward measure,

$$
\mathbb{P}_Z\bigl(G^{-1}(C)\bigr)
= G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z(C)
= 1.
$$

By continuity of $G$, $G^{-1}(C)$ is closed in $\mathcal{Z}$. Then

$$
G^{-1}(C) \in \mathcal{C}(\mathbb{P}_Z),
$$

and, by the definition of support,

$$
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z) \subseteq G^{-1}(C).
$$

Applying $G$ to both sides, and using that $C \in \mathcal{C}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z)$, gives

$$
G\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\bigr)
\subseteq \operatorname{supp}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z).
$$

Applying closure on both sides:

$$
\operatorname{cl}\!\left(G\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\bigr)\right)
\subseteq \operatorname{cl}\!\left(\operatorname{supp}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z)\right)
= \operatorname{supp}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z).
$$

According to the pushforward measure,

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z\!\left(
  \operatorname{cl}\!\left(G\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\bigr)\right)
\right)
&= \mathbb{P}_Z\!\left(
  G^{-1}\!\left(G\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\bigr)\right)
\right) \\
&= \mathbb{P}_Z\!\left(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\right) \\
&= 1.
\end{aligned}
$$

So this leads to the conclusion that

$$
\operatorname{supp}(G_\#\mathbb{P}_Z)
= \operatorname{cl}\!\left(G\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_Z)\bigr)\right).
$$

### 3.4 Experiments

Two experiments were conducted to verify this hypothesis:

1. **Disjoint DGM training:** split the datasets class-wise and compare performance, using FID score on the test set, to argue that the datasets are disjoint.
2. **Varying intrinsic dimensions:** show that the disjoint subsets have different dimensions. This is not covered in this note.

## Results

For experiment 1, they noted that FID scores (lower is better) were better for disjoint models than their fully trained counterparts across MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100.

## Limitations

The proof and experiments hinge on the hypothesis. In my view, the experiments did not present a strong case for it.

It remains to be seen how this works for other modalities—video, text, and audio—and for bigger datasets with more classes, such as ImageNet.

They themselves state that classes are a good approximation of connected components, but they do not match exactly and cause inconsistencies.
